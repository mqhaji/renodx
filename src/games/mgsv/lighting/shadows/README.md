# Direct lighting

Shadows selects Vanilla or Enhanced. Enhanced combines PCSS filtering of native shadow maps with Bend screen-space contact shadows. Both affect direct diffuse and specular lighting inside the game's light draws, before GT-VBGI, skin scattering, and deferred composition. Environment reflections, native material masks, distance fades, and output alpha retain their original roles. Vanilla restores native shadows.

## Coverage and placement

The [shader registry](../../shaders.hpp) covers all 29 direct-light variants identified in the local dump: five sun/moon variants, 16 spotlight variants, and eight point-light variants. This includes projected masks, diffuse-only lighting, both distance-falloff families, and lights without shadow maps. Shadowed lights use PCSS and Bend; unshadowed lights use Bend alone. The largest-cascade sun shader retains its separate world-shadow mask. Shaders absent from the dump have not been qualified.

The ambient skylight shader, `SSLighting2_SH_SkyLight_Encode_ps` (`0x4FBDBB39`), has a separate role. It samples directional sky illumination and blends it with native diffuse accumulation, then applies the native SH mask. It has no direct-light shadow map or single source direction for PCSS or Bend. Its existing callback identifies ambient inputs for GT-VBGI's diffuse AO; Enhanced shadows do not darken this contribution again.

The [host](screen_space_shadows.hpp) runs [setup](contact/bend_setup.cs_5_0.hlsl), a bounded [clear](contact/bend_clear.cs_5_0.hlsl), and a depth [trace](contact/bend_trace.cs_5_0.hlsl). All local lights of a lighting pass share one run of these dispatches; the sun runs them once per pass. Each native light draw then [combines](screen_space_shadows.hlsli) contact visibility with its shadow-map result before coloring the light. Missing contact inputs leave shadow-map lighting available. Unsupported replacement bindings retain the original shader.

## Shadow-map filtering

[PCSS](pcss.hlsli), or percentage-closer soft shadows, searches the native shadow map for blockers, estimates their distance from the receiving surface, and filters visibility over a corresponding soft edge. Edges stay tight near contact and widen with caster separation. Base source sizes are 0.27 degrees for sun/moon and 0.05 game units for local lights, scaled by Shadow Softness. These are chosen values; physical light sizes and game units in meters have not been measured.

- Sun/moon filtering follows the native four-cascade atlas. It selects a coarser cascade when needed to contain the bounded search and keeps samples inside that tile.
- Spotlights use the full perspective matrix to reconstruct blocker distance without treating camera rotation or translation as a change in softness.
- Point lights retain the native dual-paraboloid projection. Samples choose their hemisphere independently, including across the seam.

Receiver-slope comparisons reduce self-shadowing. The sun filter also rejects a slope adjustment that could erase a detected blocker. Searches and final radii are capped at 0.25 game units. A missed blocker, a filter radius under one shadow-map texel, or an unavailable projection retains the original four-tap filter: those taps sit about 0.73 texel out, wider than a disk under one texel spreads its own, so below that the native filter is the softer of the two and a soft edge never gets sharper as its radius grows. The native light shaders square the map visibility they are given, a shaping of their four-tap result that would put the middle of a soft edge at a quarter of the light and move the edge toward the lit side. PCSS hands them the square root of its result, so the light a soft edge receives is the share of the source it sees. Those taps run only when needed. A fully blocked contact result can skip map filtering, except when diagnostics require it. [Native lighting notes](NATIVE_LIGHTING.md) describe the packing, projections, and measured contracts.

### Sampling and reconstruction

PCSS uses golden-angle disk directions with square-root radial spacing. The shared IS-FAST texture rotates the disk and shifts samples within their radial bands: a 128-by-128 tile with 32 temporal layers, uploaded once per device and reused with GT-VBGI Off. Sampling is fixed at 12 sun/moon blocker samples, four local blocker samples, and four filter samples.

The selected swapchain's Present bookkeeping advances the noise layer. This moves shadow-map samples within the filter disk; camera jitter separately offsets the camera projection. Later temporal reconstruction helps stabilize the resulting lighting. There is no dedicated shadow history or denoising pass, and moving objects or newly revealed surfaces can still expose noise. The pattern also animates with reconstruction Off.

## Contact shadows

[Bend's method](https://www.bendstudio.com/blog/inside-bend-screen-space-shadows/) shares camera-depth reads between neighboring receivers. Native maps supply offscreen caster coverage; Bend supplies fine visible detail. Conservative light bounds and cone/range checks skip unlit receivers while retaining the casting depths needed by nearby lit pixels; a ray group with no receiver to trace for (sky alone, or pixels a local light does not reach) reads no more depths, and pixels a local light does not reach stay as the clear left them, which the light shaders take as unshadowed. Spotlight evaluations share a GPU budget; point and directional lights do not consume it. The budget resets beside native lighting-buffer clears, including deferred command-list replay. Within a batch it goes to the highest-ranked lights (below); across the batches of one lighting pass it counts evaluations in GPU order.

### Local-light batches

The [local-light hook](../../engine/local_lights.hpp) runs when the engine's local-light plugin begins on its build thread, before it branches the jobs that draw unshadowed and shadowed lights. There the [batch](batch.hpp) cuts the build thread's command list and reserves its dispatches at that point. The engine executes that list before every light job's list, and only after all of them have been recorded.

Every local light draw that writes lighting, with or without a shadow map, then adds its light to the batch: the CPU constants its worker has just uploaded, its type, and for shadowed lights its shadow-map projection. The batch uploads all lights when its dispatches execute, so lights recorded after the cut are included. All lights of a batch share depth, lighting target, settings, and camera; the first light fixes them.

Setup builds every light's record in parallel, ranks the lights, then admits them by rank: each takes atlas space for its receiver bounds, and spotlights count against the budget. A light's rank comes from how much light it puts on the surfaces in view: its native falloff (with the limits that bound it at the source) and cone at an 8 × 8 grid of pixels across its receiver bounds, read against the scene depth, times its color and the bounds' area. So when a scene has more spotlights than the budget, contact shadows go to the lights that shape the picture (a lamp on a face in a close-up) before lamps far away or off to the side; lights of equal rank admit in draw order. Point lights take no budget but are ranked with the spots for atlas space. Three limits follow from the method. A light whose lit patch falls between the grid's pixels scores nothing and admits after every light that scored; this is likeliest for a light whose bounds cover the whole screen. A large light is admitted before several small ones of less total effect, so when the atlas and not the budget is the limit, it can serve fewer lights than draw order did. And the rank is taken from each frame's depth and colors, so a light near the budget's edge can gain or lose its contact shadows as the camera, a character or a flickering lamp changes the picture. Clear and trace then process all admitted rectangles in parallel, and each light draw reads its own record and packed rectangle. The clear is required because the trace skips ray bundles that miss the light's range and leaves their texels unwritten. Replaying a command list recomputes the masks from that execution's depth; uploaded inputs and GPU resources remain alive until their recorded work is retired.

The sun records a one-light batch beside its first draw of a pass, copying its constants from that draw's bound buffers; the pass's later sun draws reuse its mask. A local light the frame batch cannot take records a one-light batch the same way and logs the reason once. One atlas allocation stays below 256 MiB and the D3D11 texture-size limit, and its width is a power of two, so the light shaders turn a mask's texel index into atlas coordinates without dividing. It holds 13 screens of masks, or as many as the spotlight budget, and a light that no longer fits keeps its shadow-map result. On an executable whose hooks cannot be verified, Enhanced shadows keep the native light shaders.

### Soft contact edges

Bend traces toward the native light center. The first solid caster determines a soft-edge estimate using the same source sizes as PCSS. Perspective projection reduces that calculation to screen coordinates. The existing mask stores visibility in R and support plus filter radius in G. The light shader applies a normalized, depth-aware 3-by-3 Gaussian filter only where visibility changes and the radius reaches half a render pixel. It rejects separate surfaces and thin depth peaks, with a maximum radius of two render pixels. A shadowed pixel whose radius is larger (a shadow far from its caster, up to 12 render pixels) takes eight taps over a disk of that radius instead, turned by a different angle at each pixel and frame so temporal reconstruction fills the disk in. That filter runs on shadowed pixels only, so its soft edge lies inside the shadow: where lit taps hold half the weight, the pixel is lit, and lit pixels beside it stay lit. Over the first render pixel past the small filter's radius both sides change over gradually, so an edge does not jump where its radius crosses two pixels. A shadow narrower than its soft edge is lost rather than spread beyond its outline: a band 8 render pixels wide at a radius of 12 keeps about a sixth of its darkening. With the tuned lamp radius of 0.05 game units the radius seldom passes a few pixels (about 3 at the edge of a chest's shadow on an arm in a Mother Base cutscene close-up); in a test with a lamp radius of 0.2 the same edge became a smooth gradient. Uniform and subpixel contacts keep their raw visibility. This adds no pass, texture, or history buffer.

This bounded filter approximates a finite source; it is not an exact area-light integral. Map and contact visibility are still multiplied. Hidden geometry is unavailable, and thin geometry can change coverage with resolution or camera jitter. Longer reach extends the search with sparser far samples (below), so it can expose more screen-space errors. Bend keeps its source centered; only the wide soft edge turns from frame to frame.

Fixed trace settings are contrast 4, 0.5% relative nonlinear-depth thickness for the contact ramp, a caster thickness from the caster's width up to 0.3 game units, a 2% bilinear depth threshold, four hard-contact samples, four interleaved soft-sample minima, eight end-fade samples, and 64 threads per group. Each group keeps 320 casting depths along its ray, counted in render pixels along the ray's main screen axis from its first receiver toward the light. The reference method keeps every pixel, which held the reach at 256 pixels: in a 4K close-up about 10 cm, where a chest's shadow on an arm ended along a curve 240 pixels from the chest (seen in a Mother Base cutscene). Here the first 128 are every pixel, the next 64 every second pixel and the rest every fourth, so the same number of depth reads reaches 704 pixels, and in that cutscene the shadow runs to its real edge. A receiver tests the kept depths between itself and its reach in turn, so its nearest 64 pixels or more are tested one by one and contacts keep their detail. A kept depth that stands for two pixels feeds two of the four interleaved minima and one that stands for four feeds all four, as at reduced render scale, so a caster darkens a receiver as much in the sparser part as in the dense part; a caster thinner than the spacing there can still fall between the kept pixels, or darken fully where the reference method would darken half. All sample groups run at the current scene resolution. The sample counts and the neighbor-depth bias are tuned in output pixels: below about 71% render scale the trace uses two hard samples, two minima, and four fade samples, and below about 35% one hard sample and one minimum, while the bias scales directly with render scale. Without that, thin casters lost most of their shadow in upscaled modes. Casters thinner than a render pixel still appear in only some jittered frames, so their accumulated shadow stays lighter than at native resolution. Optional edge discard, precision offset, ray-aligned sampling, and group depth-bound early-out are disabled.

Depth shows only a caster's front surface, so the trace has to assume how far back it is solid. The reference method uses one thickness for both the contact ramp and that assumption, as a share of the distance from the camera: 0.5%, which is 0.15 game units at 30 units but about 0.01 in a close-up. There a chest a few hundredths of a unit in front of an arm stopped casting on the pixels beside it, and the shadow started a gap away from its caster, broken into wedges along the trace's 64-pixel groups (seen on a character's arm in a Mother Base cutscene, and gone with a fixed caster thickness of 0.05, 0.15 or 0.3 units in that scene). A fixed 0.1 units in turn made each lock of hair cast on the face as a slab that deep. The ramp keeps the reference thickness, so fine contacts stay sharp; a caster is then taken to be as thick as it is wide along the light ray: a ray that passes some distance behind its front surface is blocked when the caster still covers the ray that far to either side along it, up to 0.3 game units behind, and never less than the reference. In that scene this kept the arm's shadow whole and removed the slabs from the face. A wide, thin sheet that hangs clear of a surface, such as a flag, still counts as thick; the units are measured at the receiver's depth, so they are approximate for casters much nearer the camera than their receiver.

## Controls

| Control | Default | Effect |
|---|---:|---|
| Shadows | Enhanced | Selects native shadows or enhanced shadow-map filtering and contact shadows together |
| Shadow Softness | 100% | Scales the shared source sizes; 0% keeps native map filtering and sharp contact detail |
| Contact Shadow Reach | 0.20 | Shared search length toward the light in game units, from 0.05 to 2; each pixel converts it to screen pixels at its own depth and for the light's direction, between 16 and 704 render pixels |
| Spot Contact Shadow Budget | 34 | Limits spotlight contact evaluations between lighting-buffer clears; 0 is unlimited |

Softness, reach, budget, and diagnostics are in Direct Lighting (Advanced), collapsed by default. A local light's center sits inside its lamp, so each trace toward a light in front of the camera stops 0.25 game units short of it; closer casters are taken to be the light's own fixture, which the engine's shadow maps leave out too, and receivers that close to the light stay lit. Because reach is a world distance along the light's direction, a contact shadow keeps its length as the camera moves closer or farther or turns; very close to a surface the 704-pixel limit shortens it, and far away, or under a light nearly along the view, the 16-pixel minimum lengthens it. The length was first taken across the view, which searched farther than stated toward a light near the view axis (about three times at a low sun ahead of the camera); the default rose from 0.1 to 0.2 with the correction to keep contact shadows about as long as they were in such views. The earlier pixel reach (`DirectLightingContactShadowReachPixels`) is no longer read; its 60 pixels were only about 2 cm at the ACC title's close camera. Longer reach costs more where the camera is close, since each pixel then needs more samples. Reach changes search distance, not softness. Skipped spotlight contacts retain shadow-map filtering. Preset Off selects Vanilla for Shadows; Reset All restores defaults.

## Diagnostics and verification

Both debug controls use Debug Light to select Sun / Moon, Spot, or Point. Diagnostics affect the selected light draw, so surface color, other lighting, GI, fog, and later processing can still affect the image. Magenta marks unavailable inputs or coordinates.

- Shadow Map Debug View shows Blocker Distance, Filter Radius, Map Visibility, or Region. Map Visibility is the value handed to the native shader, before its square. Distance uses `distance / (1 + distance)`; radius is relative to its fixed maximum. Sun cascades are red, green, blue, and yellow; point hemispheres are red and green.
- Contact Shadow Debug View shows Visibility, Depth Edges, Ray Layout, Without Contact Shadows, Shadow Detail, or Toward Light. Without Contact Shadows keeps the calculations running for comparisons. Shadow Detail marks evaluated areas. Arrows point toward the native light; cast shadows extend away. The visible moon alone does not establish the selected lighting direction; see the [controller notes](NATIVE_LIGHTING.md#sun-and-moon-controller).

Follow the [addon build and deployment checks](../../README.md#build-and-deploy). Turn diagnostics Off for appearance and performance comparisons.

1. Compare Vanilla/Enhanced at a fixed camera, then move the camera and characters. Check contact edges, broad shadows, foliage, fences, slopes, cascades, spotlight rims, and point-light seams.
2. Compare softness at 0%, 50%, and 100%, including the ACC menu. Check different reconstruction resolutions, startup, resize, odd dimensions, and native shadow-quality settings. Thin beard geometry can still show instability.
3. At Mother Base at night, compare spotlight budgets 13 and 0. Confirm omitted contacts retain map shadows. Check daylight and moonlight separately, using Toward Light to identify the source when needed.

In-game checks at Mother Base at night, at Native and DLSS Performance: one batch covered all 495 local light draws of a pass, including 16 shadowed spotlights; each traced light's record matched the projection from its draw's GPU constants within 0.0001 render pixel; and the parallel setup admitted the same lights, with the same bounds, as the earlier serial setup. The outdoor guardrail/moon direction comparison remains unverified. Use a fixed-camera Enhanced / Vanilla / Enhanced comparison for performance, with debug views and capture copies disabled.
