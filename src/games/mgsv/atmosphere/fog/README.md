# Fog volume

The game keeps its fog in one image, the fog volume: 128 depth slices as a 32 × 4 grid of tiles, with the light scattered toward the camera in RGB and the transmittance in alpha. It fills the volume with one draw at the start of each view, and about 170 shaders read it afterward: deferred composition for opaque surfaces, then the sky, particles, water and other forward draws. The game's fill gives every cell the light of an unshadowed path, so its fog glows the same in shade and in sun.

The mod does two things. The volume is always created larger. With **Fog** set to Enhanced, the main view's volume is filled again with the sun's and moon's shadows in it: nearby from the cascade shadow maps, and across the map from the terrain. Vanilla keeps the game's fill.

## The larger volume

`engine/render_targets.hpp` changes the volume's creation size from 512 × 256 (16 × 64 cells per slice) to 5120 × 360 (160 × 90). No shader that reads the volume needs to change: each addresses a tile by fractions of it, and the vertex shader that fills it places cells by fractions too. The fill runs under a viewport fixed at 512 × 256, so `OnVolumeDraw` widens that viewport to the image's size for the draw and `OnVolumeDrawn` puts it back.

The game's readers take one bilinear lookup in each of two slices, and at 1/160 of the view's width per cell that shows the grid as fog moves across the screen: a lamp's glow changes shape from cell to cell as the camera turns. All 32 ported shaders that read the volume (deferred composition, local reflections, the sky, clouds, the ocean and the lit particles) read it through `FogVolumeSample` (`fog_read.hlsli`) instead: a cubic B-spline over 4 × 4 cells in four bilinear lookups, kept inside the slice's tile. It smooths slightly and adds no detail, and it applies with Fog on Vanilla too, since some of those ports take no injected settings. The game's shaders that are not ported (forward-drawn objects among them) keep the bilinear lookup.

Slice s lies at view depth 2^(s / 128 / k), with k in `m_fogParam[1].x`; the engine sets k so the last slice is where the fog's transmittance reaches a fixed small value. RGB is stored divided by `m_fogParam[1].y` and clamped to 1; readers multiply by it.

## Enhanced

The fog stays the game's: the same scattering terms, lobe, sky term, self-luminance and rain-shelter lookup, evaluated with the fog draw's constants. What changes is that sunlight reaches a cell only where the shadow maps, the cloud shadow and the terrain let it, and that the stored light is not clamped to 1. Where every cell is lit, the game's clamp does not cut, no local light reaches, the fog is thin enough to stay even and Fog Height Falloff is 0, the result equals the game's volume.

The game divides the light by a scale it picks per time of day (`m_fogParam[1].y`, from 0.08 at night to 0.84 at a cloudy noon in the captures) and clamps the result to 1. The scale keeps the light under 1 most of the time, but not at a low sun: at 06:25 in Afghanistan the haze beyond about 2 km went past 1 in every column (up to 1.21), and at sunrise at Mother Base in 5% of the far columns (up to 1.41), so the game showed the sunrise glow in the haze flattened. The volume is a 16-bit float image and every reader in the decompiled dump (136 pixel shaders) either multiplies the stored light by the scale with no clamp or reads only the transmittance, so the mod stores the light unclamped. Noon, sunset and night in clear weather never reached 1.

The fog's color and strength are not taken from the light surfaces get. Measured at the title screen, the sun's irradiance on surfaces is about three times the sun strength the fog material carries, and the sky light is five to ten times the fog material's sky color; fog lit by them is far brighter than the sky the game draws behind it. The fog material's values are the ones that agree with that sky.

### Placement and inputs

The passes run on D3D11 just before the first skin-scatter pass (`0x6DAF0C4A`): the sun's shadow maps are drawn by then, and deferred composition, the volume's first reader, comes after. They run for the presented main view only; other views keep the game's fill.

| Input | Source |
|---|---|
| Fog constants | The fog draw's (`VolFog_TppVolFog`, `0x99BEE81F`) pixel constants at b0, b2, b4 and b5: system, scene, fog material, and the eye position and sun direction |
| Rain-occlusion map | The fog draw's t0; one lookup at the camera weakens the forward lobe under an authored shelter |
| Cascade shadow maps, cloud shadow, world shadow mask | The shadowed sun or moon draw's t3 and t4 (`SunLight_DL_CS`, `SunLight_DL_CS_ES`, `SSLighting2_Sun_LargestCascade`), and t13 for the last |
| Cascade matrices, shadow range, cloud shadow parameters | The same draw's pixel constants at b2, b3 and b5 |
| Terrain tile map and height atlas | A terrain depth or shadow draw's vertex-stage t0 and t1 (`NewTerrain_Depth`, `0x4710870E`; `NewTerrain_Shadow`, `0xD68FF97A`; and the `_ln` variant of each, `0xFC72369A` and `0xEAF0D644`), in any view, refreshed a few times a second |
| The map's tiling, place and size | The same draw's vertex constants at b4 (first vector) and b5 |

Constants are copied on the GPU inside the draw's command list, so a replayed list copies them again and the passes read the values of the frame being drawn. A view without a fog draw gets no pass. A view without a shadowed sun draw gets no shadow from the maps, and a map without terrain (Mother Base, interiors) none from the ground; with neither, every cell is lit. A main view build that records a terrain draw is marked as having terrain, and stays so in every replay of its lists, so the terrain shadows neither drop out nor come back while the game replays them. The terrain textures are kept until another map's terrain replaces them; a map without terrain builds no view so marked. The engine's other fill shader, `Fog_DrawVolume` (`0xD76ECA29`), reads none of the fog material; a view filled by it keeps the game's fog.

### The passes

1. **Terrain heights** (`fog_terrain_heights.cs_5_0.hlsl`), only where the map has terrain. The engine keeps the ground as pages of 16-bit heights in an atlas: full detail around the player, and coarser pages for the rest of the map. The pass looks a height up as the terrain vertex shader does (the tile map gives a tile's page and level) for 256 × 256 points across the map, which is every height of the coarsest level.
2. **Terrain shadow** (`fog_terrain_shadow.cs_5_0.hlsl`). For each of those points, the height below which the ground toward the light hides it: each texel walks across the height image toward the light, and ground n steps away shades everything below its height less n times the light ray's climb per step. The walk ends at the map's edge or once the map's highest ground could no longer reach above the shadow found. A point in the air is then lit when it is above the stored height at its place on the map, however far away the hill is. The distance to the ground that casts the shadow is stored beside it. Both terrain passes run every frame, on 256 × 256 texels.
3. **Visibility** (`fog_visibility.cs_5_0.hlsl`), one thread per cell. A cell covers its column between its slice and the one before. Each frame it asks two things at four points in the cell.
   - *The shadow maps*, as the shadowed sun shaders compute a surface's shadow there: the first cascade whose square holds the point and whose depth range does not lie toward the light from it, one comparison lookup where the game takes four, squared as the game squares its filtered result; faded out toward the sphere where the game ends its shadows (to lit, or for `SSLighting2_Sun_LargestCascade` to its world shadow mask); times the cloud shadow. Beyond the last cascade, where the game lights surfaces without shadow maps, the point is lit. The terms those shaders add that do not shadow (a visibility offset, a fade with view depth, a least visibility for specular light) are left out, so fog in open sun stays the game's. Around dawn and dusk the shadow maps belong to one body (the moon, or a sun the engine holds well above the horizon) while the game lights the fog from another direction (the rising or setting sun), so the maps' shadows apply in full only while the two directions are within 30 degrees and not at all beyond 45.
   - *The terrain shadow*, one lookup. It is cast along the direction the game lights the fog from, so it holds at dawn and dusk too. A point at or above the shadow's top is fully lit, so fog over open ground stays the game's; below the top the shadow fades in over 8 units of height plus the sun's width in the sky times the distance to the ground that casts it. Around sunrise the game lights the fog from a sun held on the horizon (seen at 06:18 in Afghanistan); a light at or below the horizon is taken as on it, so everything below the highest ground toward it is in shadow. Past the map's edge nothing shades a point.

   The points move every frame along low-discrepancy sequences (Roberts' R2 across the cell and R3 over time) with an offset per cell. The result is one frame's estimate.
4. **Integration** (`fog_volume.cs_5_0.hlsl`), one thread per screen column. From the camera outward, each cell first blends its estimate with the value the previous frame's visibility image holds at the same place in the world, one twentieth new; a cell with no previous value takes the estimate whole. Before blending, the old value is kept within the range of this frame's estimates in the 27 cells in and around the cell. Where a shadow has just arrived or left (something moved), those estimates agree, and the old value gives way in that frame; where they are noisy, the old value steadies them. Then each slab between two slices adds its source over the scattering coefficient times the drop in transmittance across it, which is exact for the game's curve of optical depth over distance. The sun's part of the source is scaled by the cell's visibility. The result is copied into the engine's volume.

Only visibility is carried over time. The fog's light is evaluated fresh each frame, so a change of weather or time of day does not trail.

### What is kept and what is corrected

The rule (the user's, 2026-11-21): keep the game's fog parameters except where the game's model is not physically accurate. Kept: the fog's amount per weather and time and its curve over distance, its colors, the sun's strength in it, the lobe, the sky-lit share and the sky color. Corrected so far: sunlight reaches fog only where nothing shades it (shadow maps, terrain); the stored light is not clamped; lamps light the fog, behind their shadows where the game gives them shadow maps; the density thins with height; thick fog is uneven and drifts; lit particles the game draws without sun shadows take the fog's. Left as the game's although not physical, because correcting them changes the look of every weather and belongs with the physical sky: the optical depth grows as a power of distance below 1 where a real medium's grows linearly; only the forward and blue shares scatter sunlight and only the gray share scatters sky light, where every part of a real medium scatters both; the forward lobe carries a constant share that is not normalized.

### Height

The game's fog is as thick at 750 units of height as at 190. Here its density falls off as exp(-height / scale height) above a reference: the map's lowest ground from the terrain draw's constants, or zero (sea level) on a map without terrain. The game's density holds at the reference. Each slab's optical thickness on the game's curve is multiplied by the falloff's mean along the slab, in closed form, and the transmittance and every light term use the result. **Fog Height Falloff** at 100% uses a scale height of 1,200 units, a typical value for haze in the lower atmosphere; 0% is the game's even fog. The sky draw (`Sky_Draw_TppBaked`) scales the far slice by a factor from its material before applying it; what that factor follows is not traced, and the sky with the falloff on has not been looked at in game.

### Unevenness

Thick fog and blowing dust are never even, so where the game's fog is dense its density is varied by a noise that drifts with a fixed wind: banks (a noise texel per 6 units) and wisps (per 1.5 units), each moving at its own pace so the pattern changes as it moves. The variation is around the game's density as the mean. How dense is read from the fog's range, the distance where the game's fog turns opaque (`m_fogParam[1].x`): none above 5,000 units, full below 1,000. In the captured weathers that is none on clear, cloudy and rainy days (ranges of 6,500 units and more), a little in rain and fog at night (3,700), and full in daytime fog (790) and sandstorms (84). Each slab reads the noise at its middle, blurred to the slab's length, so long slabs far away take the mean. The noise is a 64-texel volume that repeats in every direction, made on the CPU once. Time is the host's clock, so the fog also drifts while the game is paused. **Fog Unevenness** scales the variation; 0% is the game's even fog. At 100% the density runs from almost none to twice the game's and its mean stays the game's (1.002 times it, from a replica of the noise).

### Local lights

The game lights its fog with the sun or moon and the sky only. Enhanced adds the spot and point lights of the view. They have no part in the game's fog to keep, so the fog scatters their light as a medium of its density does. All three of the fog material's terms scatter it, each spreading what it scatters over all directions with nothing added: the forward term by its lobe, less its constant share, which goes evenly in every direction; the blue term by its own angle dependence; the third, which the game lights from the sky, evenly. The forward term's color is authored to tint the sun's white strength, so a lamp takes its largest channel and keeps its own color; in a weather whose forward color is strongly tinted, the fog then scatters more of a lamp's light in the weaker channels than the game's transmittance takes from the view there. The light arriving at a point of the fog is π times what the light shaders put on a surface facing the lamp there (color times falloff times cone, exposed), less what the fog between the lamp and that point takes. Lamps therefore keep their brightness in the fog against their light on surfaces at any exposure. The game's sun strength in the fog is authored for each weather and time and does not follow the exposure, so lamps against the sun in the fog vary between scenes as the exposure does.

- **The lights** are the ones Enhanced shadows gather at the light draws for contact shadows (`lighting/shadows/batch.hpp`): each light's constants, its kind, and the falloff limits of its material constants. That batch fills its light buffer before the lights draw, which is before the fog's passes, so the fog reads the same buffer. With Shadows set to Vanilla nothing is gathered and the fog has no local lights; the setting is disabled and says so.
- **Bins** (`fog_light_bins.cs_5_0.hlsl`). Mother Base at night draws about 500 lights, so they are sorted first: one thread per block of 8 × 8 cells by 4 slices keeps the 12 lights that light the block most among those whose reach (and for a spot, cone) touches it. A thread group's 64 blocks lie side by side at one depth or two and most lights reach only a few depths, so the group first lists the lights that reach the box around all its blocks, and each thread tests only that list. Choosing by strength keeps a crowded block's choice steady from frame to frame.
- **Per cell** (in `fog_volume.cs_5_0.hlsl`). A lamp's falloff is far too steep to sample at a point, so its inverse-square part is integrated in closed form along the cell's stretch of the view ray (the integral of 1 / (h² + s²) is atan(s / h) / h), with the game's near-source limits as a least distance from the ray. A cell's light stands for every ray through the cell, so the lamp's distance from the ray is first replaced by the one whose falloff is the mean over the cell's width where the lamp is nearest (a disk as large as the cell's section, in closed form within 2.2%), and the game's limits apply after that, so no cell is brighter than they allow; taking the middle ray alone made a lamp's glow swing by up to three times as the camera turned and the lamp slid between rays. In terms of the angle between the view ray and the direction to the lamp, that falloff weighs every angle alike, so the scattering's dependence on the angle is integrated over the angle in closed form as well; four points spread along the ray instead had made a lamp's own cell up to three times too bright. With 32-bit floats and the arctangent as the GPU evaluates it, the lobe's mean is within 0.3% of a dense integral for lobes up to the sharpest captured (sharpness 0.958), and at the sharpest the game allows (0.9999, where the lobe matters only toward the lamp itself) the lamp's whole scattered light is within 0.5%. The fade toward the light's reach, a spot's cone, its shadow and the fog's toll between the lamp and the ray are taken at four angles spread evenly over the stretch.
- **Reach.** A light is followed to where the game's falloff ends, or sooner, to where its light on a surface would fall under 0.004 in exposed units; it fades toward that distance on the game's falloff curve.
- **Shadows.** The game renders every shadowed spot light's shadows into one 4096 × 4096 map, one light after another, so by the time the fog runs only the last light's are left. At each shadowed spot's draw in the main view, `OnShadowedSpotDraw` therefore copies the map, reduced to 512 × 512 (`fog_spot_shadow.cs_5_0.hlsl`), into a tile of a 16-tile atlas, and the light's position and shadow projection into the tile's record, all in the draw's command list. The bins pass matches a light to its tile by position, and each of the four points of a cell's stretch is tested as the light's draw tests a surface: through the projection, lit where no stored depth is nearer the light. The reduced map keeps one depth of each 8 × 8 block, so an occluder narrower than about 8 of the map's texels can be missed or flicker. Lights the game draws without a shadow map, point lights, and shadowed spots past the sixteenth light the fog on both sides of a wall.
- **Not yet:** spot lights with a projected mask shine unmasked. The game's painted light cones and glow sprites are untouched and add to this.

### Particles

The game lights its lit particles (smoke, dust, spray) with the sun or moon, an ambient pair and local lights (one in `LitDP1`, read; two and three in `LitDP2` and `LitDP3` by their names; none in `LitD_Fast`). Eight of those shaders, the `NS` and `Fast` variants of `Prim_Poly_Lit*` that read the fog volume, add the sun's light without its shadow maps (their sun term is the sun draw's color, equal to four digits in a capture), so smoke in a building's or a hill's shadow glows like smoke in the open. Their ports (`effects/particles/`) multiply that one term by the fog's sun visibility at the particle: `fog_particle.hlsli` looks it up in a copy of the visibility image the host binds at t22 for the main view's draws (`OnParticleDraw`). Without the image (Fog on Vanilla, another view, the first frames) the game's shader stays. The ports are the decompiled shaders with that edit, written by `port_particles.py` in the fog analysis folder. Unlit particles (`Softblend`, `FastDraw`) bind no light constants; their color comes from the texture and a per-particle color, and they are unchanged.

### Settings

Under **Atmosphere (Advanced)**, shown while Fog is Enhanced:

- **Fog Shadow Strength** (`AtmosphereFogShadowStrength`, default 100%): how much of the sun's light a shadow takes from the fog. 0% gives the game's fog.
- **Fog Height Falloff** (`AtmosphereFogHeightFalloff`, 0 to 100%, default 100%): how fast the density thins with height. 0% is the game's even fog.
- **Fog Unevenness** (`AtmosphereFogUnevenness`, 0 to 100%, default 100%): how much thick fog's density varies. 0% is the game's even fog.
- **Local Lights in Fog** (`AtmosphereFogLocalLights`, 0 to 100%, default 100%): the strength of the local lights' share. Disabled unless Shadows is Enhanced.
- **Fog Terrain Shadows** (`AtmosphereFogTerrainShadows`, default On): the terrain passes and their lookup.
- **Fog Debug View** (`AtmosphereFogDebugView`): Shadows writes each cell's visibility into the volume in place of its light, over a dimmed scene. Local Lights writes only the local lights' share.

### Limits

- The shadow maps' shadows end where the cascades end, as they do on the ground: under 100 units. Farther away only the terrain shades the fog: buildings, rocks and trees placed on the ground do not, and neither do clouds or the mountains drawn beyond the map's edge.
- The terrain shadow knows the ground at 256 points a side (32 units apart on the game's 8 km maps), so it suits haze seen over hundreds of units, not a shadow's exact edge on the ground. The ground the game lights keeps the game's shadows: at a low sun the engine lights surfaces from a sun held higher, so the haze over a valley can be in a hill's shadow while the valley floor is lit.
- A shaft is as sharp as a cell allows: 1/160 of the view's width.
- The game places some lights away from their fixtures, and the fog glows around the light, not the fixture. A Mother Base floodlight's light, and the center of its shadow map's projection, sit about 0.35 units above its glass, so its glow appears above the lamp.
- A shadow smaller than a cell mostly disappears: the time filter keeps a cell's history within the range of this frame's estimates around it, and in the frames where those estimates all miss a pole or a branch the cell's partial shadow is reset.
- Height falloff keeps the game's density at the map's lowest ground, so where the player stands higher the fog is thinner than the game's: about 0.7 of it at 600 units of height in Afghanistan at the default setting.
- The sky's light is not blocked under roofs.
- Switching Fog takes effect when the game next records its command lists, like Global Illumination. Switching back to Vanilla releases the passes' images and clears their history.
- The cloud shadow term is the game's, but the game binds a 1 × 1 placeholder as its cloud texture in every scene inspected (Mother Base and Afghanistan, clear and overcast), so it changes nothing today.

## Verification

Build `mgsv`, and check the embedded shader bytes against the `fog_*.cso` files and the eight particle ports. In game, the log reports the volume's creation with 160 × 90 cells per slice and, once Enhanced runs, that the mod fills the main view's fog volume. With the local probe, capture deferred composition's fog input (pixel t12) under Vanilla and Enhanced at one camera. With Fog Height Falloff, Fog Unevenness and Local Lights in Fog at 0%, alpha is identical and RGB differs only by what shadowed cells withhold, constant along a column beyond the last shadowed slab, and by the light the game's clamp cut. At the defaults alpha differs too, because the falloff and the unevenness change the optical depth, and local lights add to RGB.
